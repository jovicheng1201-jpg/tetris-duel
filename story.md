# Milo and the Little Cloud

## A Children's Picture Book Story

### Spread 1

Every morning, Milo the little fox walked through Whispering Woods.

He waved to the tall trees.

“Good morning, trees!” he called.

He smiled at the singing birds.

“Good morning, birds!”

He even said hello to the sleepy mushrooms hiding under the leaves.

“Good morning, little mushrooms!”

Milo liked to begin each day with a kind word. He believed that a small hello could make a big difference.

Sometimes the woods answered him. The leaves whispered, “Hello.” The stream made a bright, bubbling sound. A butterfly landed on Milo’s nose as if it wanted to say good morning too.

Milo never hurried past a friend who needed help. If a beetle was upside down, Milo turned it over. If a young bird lost a feather, he helped look for it. He was small, but he always made room for kindness.

### Spread 2

One bright morning, Milo heard a tiny sound above him.

“Sniff… sniff…”

He looked up. A small white cloud was caught between two branches of an old oak tree. The cloud was round and soft, like a ball of cotton, but its little edges were trembling.

“Are you all right?” Milo asked.

“No,” whispered the cloud. “I am stuck. I am always stuck. I am the smallest and slowest cloud in the sky.”

“What is your name?”

“Puff.”

Milo climbed carefully up the tree. He pushed one branch aside, then another. At last, Puff floated free.

### Spread 3

Puff drifted a few inches into the air, then quickly sank beside Milo.

“Thank you,” said Puff. “But I should go now. The other clouds are waiting for me.”

Far above the forest, a line of large clouds moved across the blue sky. They were tall, silver, and fast. Puff watched them sadly.

“I cannot keep up,” Puff said. “The big clouds make beautiful shapes. One looks like a dragon. Another looks like a ship. I only look like a little puff.”

Milo tilted his head.

“Maybe being different is not a bad thing.”

Puff sighed. “I want to fly high, just like the others.”

“Then I will help you,” said Milo. “We will find a way together.”

Puff looked at Milo carefully. No one had ever offered to help before. Most animals only looked at the big clouds. They pointed at the shapes in the sky and forgot to notice the small cloud drifting behind them.

“What if I cannot do it?” Puff asked.

“Then we will try again tomorrow,” Milo said. “Friends do not have to be perfect. Friends just have to stay together.”

Puff felt a little warmer inside. “All right,” Puff said. “Let us begin.”

### Spread 4

First, Milo took Puff to Windy Hill, where the morning breeze danced through the grass.

“The wind can lift you,” said Milo. “Ready?”

“Ready!” Puff cried.

Whoosh!

The wind rushed up the hill. It lifted Puff into the air, spun Puff around, and carried Puff toward a bush.

Plop!

Puff landed on a bush full of purple berries.

“That was not flying,” Puff said. “That was falling with style.”

Milo laughed. Puff laughed too. Their laughter shook the berries, and three ripe berries dropped onto the grass.

“At least we found a snack,” said Milo.

They sat beneath the bush and shared the berries. The purple juice stained Milo’s whiskers, and Puff tried to clean them with a tiny puff of air. Instead, the berry juice flew onto Milo’s nose.

“Now you have a purple nose,” Puff said.

“Perhaps I am a very fancy fox,” Milo replied.

For a moment, Puff forgot about flying. Puff noticed that the journey was full of funny surprises. Maybe a journey did not have to be perfect to be good.

### Spread 5

Next, they asked three little birds for help.

“Could you pull me up?” Puff asked.

The birds tied a long vine around Puff and pulled with all their strength.

“One, two, three!” they chirped.

Pull! Pull! Pull!

Puff rose higher and higher. Milo cheered from below.

But the vine slipped from a branch.

Swoop!

Puff bounced gently onto Milo’s head.

“I am sorry,” said Puff.

“That is all right,” said Milo. “You make a very soft hat.”

The birds giggled. Puff giggled too, but when Puff looked at the high sky, the smile disappeared.

“The big clouds never fall on someone’s head,” Puff said.

“That is true,” said Milo. “But the big clouds probably cannot make a soft hat either.”

Puff floated beside him as they walked away from the nest. Above them, the big clouds stretched across the sky. They seemed to grow larger with every mile.

Puff tried to stretch too. Puff pulled its little edges wide and made a long, thin shape.

“Look!” cried Puff. “I am a ribbon!”

A gust of wind folded Puff into a ball again.

“A very beautiful ribbon,” Milo said.

### Spread 6

On the third day, Milo and Puff climbed to the top of Sunbeam Mountain.

“The mountain is very high,” Milo said. “If you start here, you may reach the sky.”

Puff looked down at the tiny trees and sparkling river.

“I am scared.”

“Me too,” Milo admitted. “But we can be scared and brave at the same time.”

Puff took a deep breath.

“One, two, three!”

Puff floated from the mountain top. For one wonderful moment, Puff rose higher than Milo’s ears.

Then the mountain wind changed.

Puff drifted slowly down and landed in a patch of warm, golden grass.

“I tried,” Puff said.

“You did,” Milo replied. “Trying is already a brave thing.”

They rested beside a warm stone. Milo opened his little travel bag and took out two acorns, a green leaf, and a piece of string.

“What are those for?” Puff asked.

“Important exploring supplies,” Milo answered. “The acorns are lunch. The leaf is a map. The string is for tying things that need tying.”

Puff looked at the very small map. It showed a crooked line, a circle for the mountain, and a large X that said, “Maybe something interesting.”

Puff smiled. Even when their plans failed, Milo always found a reason to continue.

### Spread 7

The sun climbed higher. It shone brightly over Whispering Woods.

The river became quiet and narrow. The flowers bent their heads. The rabbits searched for cool places under the ferns.

“It is very hot,” said Milo.

A little deer came out of the trees.

“Milo,” she said, “do you know where we can find water?”

Milo looked toward the dry stream. There was only a tiny puddle between two stones.

The bees buzzed tiredly. The frogs stopped singing. Even the cheerful birds were silent.

Puff floated above them. Puff wanted to help, but Puff did not know how.

“I am too small,” Puff whispered. “I cannot make a big cloud or a big storm.”

Milo looked up at his friend.

“You do not need to make a big storm. Maybe you can make one small drop.”

Milo brought Puff closer to the flowers. The yellow flower was so dry that its petals curled inward. A tiny ladybug sat beneath a leaf, hiding from the hot sun.

“I want to help,” Puff said. “But what if I cannot?”

“Then the forest will still know that you tried,” said Milo. “And I will still be here.”

The little deer, the rabbits, and the birds gathered around them. Nobody laughed at Puff. Nobody compared Puff with the large clouds. They simply waited with hopeful eyes.

Puff felt the hope moving through the forest like a gentle breeze.

### Spread 8

Puff tried to squeeze the water from inside.

Nothing happened.

Puff tried again.

Still nothing.

Puff puffed up, pushed hard, and made a very serious face.

“Come on, little cloud,” Milo said softly. “You can do this.”

Puff thought about the thirsty flowers. Puff thought about the tired rabbits. Puff thought about the silent frogs.

Then Puff felt something cool and heavy in the middle of its soft, white body.

Plink!

A single drop of water fell onto a small yellow flower.

The flower lifted its head.

“More!” cried the bees.

Puff blinked in surprise.

“I made rain!”

The first drop was tiny, but it was not alone for long. Puff felt a second drop, then a third. The drops gathered like little pearls inside the cloud.

“Do not hurry,” Milo said. “Let the rain come when it is ready.”

Puff relaxed. The little cloud did not push or stretch. Puff simply remembered the forest and let the cool water fall.

### Spread 9

Plink, plink, plink!

Three more drops fell.

The yellow flower opened its petals. A red poppy stood up. Then a blue bellflower lifted its little face to the sky.

“Keep going, Puff!” called Milo.

Puff squeezed again.

Drip, drip, drip!

The rain was gentle, but it spread across the forest. It filled the tiny puddle. It washed the dusty leaves. It made the air smell fresh and green.

The rabbits came out from under the ferns. The deer drank from the stream. The frogs began to sing.

“Ribbit! Ribbit!”

The birds flew through the rain, shaking their wings.

Everyone danced beneath Puff’s little shower.

Milo spun in a circle until his tail became a blur. The young deer skipped over the wet grass. The rabbits clapped their paws. Even the old tortoise came out of his shell to enjoy the cool air.

At the edge of the forest, the dry stream began to move. First it made a tiny trickle. Then it made a cheerful splash as water ran over the stones.

Puff grew a little smaller after the rain, but Puff did not feel weak. Puff felt light, peaceful, and proud.

### Spread 10

Puff could hardly believe what was happening.

“I cannot fly like the big clouds,” Puff said.

“No,” said Milo. “But look what you can do.”

Puff looked down at the forest. Every flower was standing tall. Every leaf was shining. Every animal had a happy face.

A tiny rabbit held up a leaf like an umbrella.

“Thank you, Puff!” she called.

The deer bowed her head.

“You saved our flowers.”

The birds sang, “Little cloud, little rain, you brought the forest life again!”

Puff began to glow softly in the sunshine.

The sunlight touched the raindrops and made a rainbow above the trees. It was not a giant rainbow, but its colors were bright: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and violet.

“You made a rainbow too!” Milo cried.

“I think the sunlight helped,” Puff said.

“That is what friends do,” said Milo. “They help one another make beautiful things.”

### Spread 11

Just then, the large clouds returned. They had crossed the whole sky and were racing toward the distant hills.

One large cloud shaped like a dragon saw Puff below.

“Why are you not flying with us?” the dragon cloud called.

Puff looked at Milo, then looked at the forest.

“I am helping my friends,” Puff answered.

The large clouds became quiet.

The cloud shaped like a ship sailed closer.

“You made all that rain?” it asked.

Puff nodded.

“Yes. It was only a little rain.”

“A little rain can be very important,” said the ship cloud.

The dragon cloud smiled.

“And you do not have to look like us. The sky needs every kind of cloud.”

Puff glanced at the other clouds. The dragon cloud could bring a loud thunderstorm. The ship cloud could travel far across the sky. A small round cloud near the moon could make a cool shadow for birds.

Every cloud had a different gift.

“Could I fly with you sometimes?” Puff asked.

“Of course,” said the ship cloud. “You may fly slowly. We will slow down too.”

The dragon cloud made a deep, friendly rumble. “And when the forest needs you, you may stay.”

For the first time, Puff did not feel left behind. Puff felt that there was a place in the wide sky for everyone.

### Spread 12

From that day on, Puff stopped trying to become a different cloud.

Puff still floated slowly. Puff was still small. Puff did not make giant dragons or ships in the sky.

But Puff could make soft rain for young flowers, cool drops for thirsty animals, and sparkling puddles for little frogs.

Milo and Puff visited the forest every morning.

“Good morning, trees!” called Milo.

“Good morning, birds!”

“Good morning, little mushrooms!”

And from high above, Puff sprinkled a few happy drops.

Plink, plink, plink!

Milo smiled.

Puff smiled too.

Puff finally understood that being special did not mean being the biggest or the fastest.

Sometimes, the smallest friend can make the biggest difference.

And whenever a new little cloud passed through Whispering Woods, Puff floated over to say hello.

“Are you looking for a place to belong?” Puff would ask.

If the little cloud was lonely, Puff would show it the bright river, the singing frogs, and the flowers that loved the rain.

Then Puff would introduce the new friend to Milo.

Milo would wave his tail and say, “Welcome! In this forest, there is always room for one more friend.”

**Small clouds can make big changes.**
